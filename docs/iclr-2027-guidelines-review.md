## Required confirmations and disclosures

### Participation limits and organizer disclosures

The rules require each organizer to disclose any other workshop proposals they are helping organize. Each organizer may be listed on no more than two proposals. Each speaker, panelist, or similar invitee may participate in no more than two distinct workshop proposals and must have agreed to be listed.

The draft now explicitly commits to the two-proposal limits for organizers and invitees and to disclosure of other organizing roles. This commitment does not list the actual other proposals or independently verify participant eligibility. Collect this information from all participants and disclose the organizers' other proposals. A statement that nobody has other proposals should be included only if verified.

### OpenReview profiles

The draft contains supplied IDs for all organizers and invitees, plus the reviewer nominee. Check that the authenticated submission form resolves each ID to the intended person. Earlier unauthenticated profile API requests returned HTTP 403, so validity was not independently established.

### Submission and review form

Enter the organizers, all nine invitees, and the reviewer nominee in the form's separate fields, as well as the PDF. A name or ID in the PDF alone does not populate those fields. Confirm the workshop requirements in the form. The reviewer recommendation describes a role reviewing 3–5 workshop proposals.

## Proposal statements still worth adding

### Talk titles and archival status

The guide explicitly says organizers should articulate how they will publish talk titles before site publication and note the archival status of submissions. The draft does not yet do this. Decide whether contributions are archival or non-archival, then add that policy and a commitment to publish talk titles and abstracts on the website before ICLR's workshop site publication.

### AI participation, if allowed

The rule to state primary AI author/reviewer participation is conditional: it applies if the workshop allows AI to serve in those roles. Absence of an AI-policy paragraph does not establish a violation of that conditional rule. Decide the policy; if those roles are allowed, explain them in the proposal.

The user approved submissions of up to four pages, explicitly welcoming one- or two-page tiny papers. The proposal and stored website guidelines now require primarily human-authored submissions, permit AI assistance, and exclude AI-generated papers under ICLR's tiny/short-paper rules.

The guide recommends following ICLR's author AI policy. A brief disclosure of actual AI assistance with proposal editing, assembly, formatting, guideline comparison, and website code is advisable. Do not claim human verification or checks that have not occurred.

### Series relationship, if applicable

If this is part of an ongoing workshop series, say so and identify the relationship. Reusing another workshop's website code alone does not establish that relationship. No series claim has been added.

## Encouraged improvements

- The approved invitation now welcomes submissions of up to four pages, including one- or two-page tiny papers with concise results, follow-up experiments, reanalyses, and early-stage work.
- Make the opportunity for junior, new, and under-resourced researchers to participate concrete through shorter submissions and the existing early-stage-work emphasis.
- The guide encourages readiness for exceptional travel constraints. The user requested omitting detailed travel arrangements; the draft retains online access to materials, which addresses the explicit content-access expectation. No promise of remote sessions is needed.

## Format and source inconsistency

The supplied guide and call, and the previously inspected live OpenReview form, do not specify a mandatory LaTeX template or a proposal page limit. The four-page policy in the draft concerns workshop contributions. The generated LaTeX has not been compiled locally, so PDF pagination is unverified.

The live form requires a PDF of at most 50 MB, title, organizers, keywords, an abstract of at most 5,000 characters, invitee IDs, reviewer recommendation IDs, workshop-requirements confirmation, and a CC BY 4.0 license. An optional TL;DR is limited to 250 characters. Submit the compiled PDF rather than the Markdown or Overleaf ZIP.

The call's introductory “April 29 and 30, 2026” conflicts with the ICLR 2027 heading and surrounding timeline. Treat it as an apparent source typo; the draft does not reproduce that event date. Verify corrected official event dates before adding them.
